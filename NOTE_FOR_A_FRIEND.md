# A note on the results, and where you could improve this

I pulled the numbers from the outreach ledger so you can see what the system has actually helped us do. In the fully tracked group, we contacted **53 people** and had **five completed coffee chats**, which works out to **9.4%**. All five chats we booked took place.

Here is the breakdown. Each person counts once in each relevant measure, even if we exchanged several messages.

| Outcome | Count | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted LinkedIn connection invitations | 11 of 48 invitation recipients | 22.9% |
| Substantive LinkedIn replies | 4 of 53 contacts | 7.5% |
| Substantive replies across LinkedIn and email | 5 of 53 contacts | 9.4% |
| Booked coffee chats | 5 of 53 contacts | 9.4% |
| Completed coffee chats | 5 of 53 contacts | 9.4% |
| Booked chats that took place | 5 of 5 bookings | 100% |

The 53 people included 48 connection-note recipients and five InMail recipients. One person responded by email after a genuine recommendation, with the original LinkedIn invitation still pending. So invitation acceptance and completed chats do not form a single funnel where every person passes through the same stages. The five InMails had no recorded substantive replies, but that sample is too small to draw much from.

These are **networking conversations**. We have no verified hiring-interview or job-offer conversion to report. The percentages describe people contacted, not the response rate for every individual message sent.

The figures come from a private ledger audit at a fixed snapshot. Some other approaches were incompletely logged; including the wider known set gives five completed chats from 57 people, or 8.8%. The public repository includes aggregate counts only. The underlying messages and records are private, so readers cannot independently reproduce the audit from this repository.

## What seemed to help

We kept the ask small, usually 15 minutes, and made the reason for reaching out clear. Researching someone's actual work also gave us better questions once we met. A genuine recommendation helped open one useful conversation, and we followed through on every booking.

There were mistakes too. Three follow-ups went out too soon and repeated the original pitch. Some introductions relied too heavily on mutual names, and some attempts at personalization sounded forced. Scheduling sometimes took more exchanges than it needed to. Later follow-ups had too little observation time to judge when the audit was done.

Those are useful lessons, but the sample cannot tell us which wording caused a reply. The people, channels, and time available to respond varied. These results also came from the earlier workflow, which included manual work, before it was packaged as this skill.

## Where you could contribute

I think there is plenty of room to improve this, especially once you adapt it to your own field. You could help with choosing whom to contact, writing more natural messages, and finding questions that give someone a real reason to talk. Making scheduling and meeting preparation more reliable across different setups would also be useful.

A better evaluation would help us learn what works over time, with enough time for replies and clear definitions of success. Synthetic examples would let us check duplicate prevention, follow-up timing, and recovery from uncertain sends without exposing anyone's messages.

Please treat this as a starting point you can shape. I would be interested in seeing what you change and what you learn from using it. Keep any real contacts and conversations in your private workspace; share generic improvements and synthetic examples here.
